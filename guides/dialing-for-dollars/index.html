Dialing for Dollars: Close a Monthly Revenue Gap in One Afternoon of Phone Calls

The end-of-month prepay script for martial arts schools. Collect tuition families already owe you, at a discount they thank you for.

A couple grand short of your monthly goal? You don't need more leads. You need a phone. Every family on your black belt path has a balance they haven't paid yet. Offer 20% off to clear it today and about 2 in 10 will say yes. When the Galvans were a couple thousand short their first month running Elk Grove alone, the third family they called had a $3,000 balance. He pulled over, gave a different card, and paid $2,400 on the spot.

The script

Six steps per call. Tap a number to tick it off.

  1. 1

    Pull the list

    Sort your active students by remaining balance. Start with families over $1,500. The bigger the balance, the bigger the savings you can offer and the bigger the check.

  2. 2

    Open the call

    Records, goal, one family, permission. "Hey Mrs. Smith, it's Master Peschke at the academy. I was going through our records, we're at the end of the month and close to a goal, and I was going to call one family to offer them substantial savings. Heck, I thought about you. Do you have a minute?"

  3. 3

    Make the offer

    20% off if they clear the balance today. Do the math out loud in dollars saved. "Right now you have a balance of $3,000. If you want to take care of that today, I can extend you a 20% discount and save you $600. Is that something you'd like to take advantage of?"

  4. 4

    One fallback, never two

    If they want it but can't swing it: "What we could do is half now and half the middle of next month. Does that help?" If that's still a no, stop. Don't stack a third offer.

  5. 5

    Land the no on a yes

    "Don't worry about a thing. My ambition is to help families like yours any time I get a chance, and I just thought about you. If I'm able to extend the savings again in the future, is it okay to let you know?" They always say yes. Then: "When will you be in class next?"

  6. 6

    Take the card and dial the next one

    Run the card on the call. A different card is fine. Expect the first two to say no thank you. Stay warm, move on. The third one might be pulling over to read you their card number.

Watch out for

  • Don't stop after two no's. The hit rate is about 2 in 10. The first two calls are supposed to be "no, thank you." That's not a signal the offer is wrong.
  • Don't lead with your problem. You mention the goal once, then the whole call is about their savings. It sounds impersonal until you realize you're helping a family keep $600.
  • Don't offer the fallback before the first no. Half-now-half-later is a rescue, not the pitch. Offer it early and you've just cut your own check in half.

FAQ

  • Isn't this pressuring families for money?

    No. You're offering 20% off money they already owe you, and they can say no. A $3,000 balance becomes $2,400. Most families who take it call back to say thank you.

  • How much of a discount should I offer?

    20% off the remaining balance, paid in full today. Say it in dollars, not percent. $150 saved on $750 sounds bigger than 20%.

  • What if they want to but can't pay the full amount?

    Half now, half the middle of next month, once. If that's still a no, land it softly and move on. Stacking offers makes you sound desperate and trains families to wait for a better deal.

  • How many calls does it take?

    Roughly 2 in 10 say yes. One yes on a $3,000 balance closes a $2,400 gap, so a short list of 10 high-balance families is usually enough to hit the month.
